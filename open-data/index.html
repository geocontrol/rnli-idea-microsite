The problem

The RNLI has open data — but it isn't really open

The RNLI's ArcGIS Open Data portal holds over 40,000 incident records (2020–2024), 238 lifeboat station locations, 254 lifeguard unit locations, and regional data. Local authorities, researchers, and civic technologists are using it. But the current licence is revocable, non-transferable, non-sub-licensable, and restricts derivative works. By the standards of the open data community, it is not open data.

📄

The fix is simple: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0

CC-BY 4.0 requires attribution only — the RNLI gets credited every time the data is used, which is free brand reach at scale. It makes RNLI data compatible with Ordnance Survey OpenData, ONS statistics, academic datasets, and virtually every civic tech tool. Moving from the current bespoke licence to CC-BY is a legal and policy decision, not a technical one. It should happen in Phase 2.

Fundraising and engagement

Data as a fundraising and mission tool

Hyperlocal supporter campaigns

"In your area, RNLI responded to 47 incidents last year." Data-driven, emotionally resonant, donor-converting. PostGIS + Superset makes this query trivial. CC-BY data makes it publishable without licence review every time.

Volunteer recruitment

Coverage gap maps showing where volunteers are most needed. Data-literate younger audiences respond to this. "Here's where we need you" is more compelling than a generic appeal. Maps the station to the person's postcode.

OCOP G1.2G2.3

Academic partnerships

CC-BY data attracts university research partnerships — water safety, climate, coastal risk. Publications generate impact and indirect funding. RNLI data is among the most comprehensive coastal incident datasets in the world.

OCOP G3.1G3.2

Policy advocacy

Open, citable incident data supports RNLI's drowning prevention advocacy with government, WHO, and UN SDG processes. Data that others can cite is more powerful than data that only the RNLI can publish.

OCOP G3.2

Civic Commons foundation

RNLI's CC-BY open data is the founding contribution to the Civic Data Commons — demonstrating RNLI's leadership and drawing in partner charities who can see that the RNLI practices what it preaches about data sharing.

OCOP G3.1

Legacy giving narrative

Long-term trend data — demand has grown fourfold in 50 years — makes a compelling case for legacy gifts. Open data that tells this story credibly, and can be cited by journalists and academics, strengthens the legacy cultivation programme.

Technical architecture

PostGIS sovereign mirror

🔧

Don't depend on Esri for your own data

The RNLI's current open data infrastructure runs on Esri ArcGIS — a proprietary, US-hosted platform. The roadmap recommends maintaining a PostGIS (PostgreSQL + spatial extension) mirror of all published datasets, hosted on UK sovereign infrastructure. This serves as: (1) a sovereign backup if Esri changes terms; (2) the data source for the internal Superset analytics platform; (3) the feed for the Civic Data Commons. The ArcGIS portal can remain as a public-facing discovery layer while PostGIS becomes the authoritative internal source. Same principle as the Linux migration: don't depend on a single US vendor for infrastructure you cannot operate independently.
