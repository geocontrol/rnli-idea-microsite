What it is

A life-saving data platform — not a surveillance tool

The Civic Data Commons is a federated, consent-based platform that allows the RNLI and partner organisations to share insight — not raw personal data — in ways that improve lifesaving outcomes. It is explicitly not a surveillance platform. Individual data about rescued persons is protected at the core. Human rights protections are architectural, not policy.

⚠️

The small boats dimension: human rights as architecture

RNLI crews rescue everyone in peril. The people on small boats crossing the Channel are in distress at sea. Some of those rescue operations have resulted in RNLI volunteers being physically attacked by those who wish to weaponise rescue data. The Commons is designed so that operational rescue data is never automatically shared with immigration enforcement. This is an architectural constraint, not a policy preference that could be overridden by political pressure.

Architecture

Four layers of data governance

Layer 1 — Protected core

Individual rescued-person data

Never shared. Retained only for immediate medical and safety need. Automatically anonymised or deleted post-incident. No immigration enforcement access under any circumstances. Legal basis: SOLAS duty to rescue; ECHR Article 2 (right to life). This layer is not part of the Commons — it exists in isolation by design.

Layer 2 — Charity commons

Aggregated insight shared with partner charities

Anonymised incident insight shared with: Black Swimming Association, RNIB, water safety NGOs, NHS ambulance trusts, refugee support charities (medical handover only, consent-based). Use case: drowning prevention, high-risk group identification, water safety campaign targeting. Federated model — RNLI does not hold partner data centrally. GDPR Article 6 lawful basis required for each data type.

Layer 3 — Civic / public layer

Open aggregated data

Published as open data under CC-BY: incident counts by area and water type, response times, seasonal demand patterns, water safety statistics. No personal data. No operational patterns that could compromise crew safety. Supports local authority planning, academic research, and WHO/UN drowning prevention advocacy.

Layer 4 — Authority interface

Strictly scoped sharing with MCA, UKHO and emergency services

Limited to operational coordination and search and rescue. Explicit legal basis required for every data type shared. Disclosed in the Commons Charter. No automatic or bulk sharing. No immigration enforcement routing. Individual court orders required — not administrative requests. Reviewed annually by an independent legal panel including a human rights barrister.

Legal framework

The immigration red line as a governance resolution

QuestionRNLI positionLegal basis
Must RNLI share rescue location data with Border Force?No automatic obligation. Rescue does not create a reporting duty under current UK law.SOLAS duty to rescue; no specific statutory reporting obligation on charities

Governing legal instruments

UK GDPR (Article 6 lawful basis for all processing) · ECHR Article 2 (positive obligation to protect life as the foundational justification) · SOLAS Convention Chapter V (international duty to render assistance) · Nationality & Borders Act 2022 (RNLI has legal clarity that rescue operations do not constitute facilitation) · Investigatory Powers Act 2016 (any bulk data request must satisfy this Act's thresholds) · Charity Act 2011 (all Commons activities must be within RNLI's charitable objects).
