Annex A

Multi-constellation GNSS: bridging the gap

GPS is not sovereign. It is operated by the US Space Force, can be jammed by cheap equipment, spoofed by increasingly accessible hardware, and selectively degraded by the US military. The RNLI's navigation posture must not depend on any single system, operator, or jurisdiction.

Hardware note: multi-constellation receivers

Modern GPS+Galileo multi-constellation receivers (u-blox M9, u-blox F9P, Septentrio Mosaic) cost little more than GPS-only equivalents. Roke Manor Research has developed a miniaturised eLoran+GNSS combined receiver under the UK-ESA NAVISP programme — this is the receiver the UK government is procuring for its eLoran programme. RNLI should monitor its commercial availability closely and register interest with the National PNT Office.

Why the SW Cornwall transmitter matters for the RNLI

The planned six sites — Anthorn, Northern Ireland, Shetlands, Outer Hebrides, East Anglia/Dover, and SW Cornwall — ring the UK coastline. The SW Cornwall site covers the Western Channel, which is the busiest small boat crossing route and where GPS jamming and spoofing incidents have been documented. RNLI should formally advocate for this site's prioritisation in the programme's coverage modelling, using its institutional standing with MCA and the General Lighthouse Authorities.

Keycloak & Nextcloud: sovereign collaboration stack

Keycloak (identity and access management) and Nextcloud (files, collaboration, communication) form the backbone of the RNLI's UK-sovereign alternative to Microsoft 365 and Azure Active Directory. Inspired by France's La Suite Numérique — deployed to 80,000+ civil servants.

Migration approach: parallel running, not big bang

Phase 1: Deploy Keycloak alongside Azure AD — federated identity, no disruption. Phase 2: Migrate HQ staff to Nextcloud + Collabora; retain M365 for 90-day parallel period. Phase 3: Shore stations migrated; volunteer-facing portal live. Phase 4: Azure AD decommissioned; full Keycloak sovereignty achieved. The France/Gendarmerie lesson: build internal competence first, mandate second.

Annex E

Crew threat model

RNLI volunteers face a threat environment that has materially changed. Crews rescuing people in the Channel have been physically targeted. Volunteers have been named on social media by hostile actors. This is not hypothetical — it has happened, and the digital dimension requires explicit modelling.

Immediate priority: social media monitoring for crew targeting

The far-right targeting of RNLI crew members via social media is the most immediate and personal threat. The CDIO must: (1) implement social media monitoring that flags content involving crew names or station locations; (2) establish a rapid response protocol with communications; (3) create a secure reporting channel for targeted crew members; (4) ensure volunteer PII is not accessible via any public-facing RNLI system. Crew members who have been targeted should have access to professional support — this is not primarily an IT problem.
