Architecture principle

Shore first, vessel second

One Crew, One Plan places enhanced vessel sensor technology in the 2030–2035 window. This roadmap builds shore infrastructure first — FreeTAK at stations, mesh nodes along the coast, OpenCPN displays, data pipelines — so that when vessel-level sensors arrive, the receiving infrastructure is already proven. 98% of RNLI launches are within 10 nautical miles of shore: the near-shore digital layer is the priority.

🛥️

RNLI's own TAK: FreeTAK as the operational nerve system

The US military's TAK (Team Awareness Kit) ecosystem has been open-sourced as FreeTAK. This gives the RNLI a fully self-hosted Common Operating Picture — real-time position reporting, incident markers, resource tracking, and inter-agency data sharing — with no dependency on US government infrastructure. FreeTAK Server runs on Linux, connects to ATAK clients on crew Android tablets, and integrates with AIS, OpenCPN, and sensor feeds.

Three-tier comms stack

No single communications dependency

Tier 1: Primary comms

VHF DSC (SOLAS standard), 4G/5G broadband data uplink, MCA Coastguard TETRA interop, AIS on all craft, Iridium satellite for blue-water operations. These are the standard operational communications — retained and integrated.

SOLASVHF DSCIridium4G/5G

Tier 2: RNLI-TAK (FreeTAK)

Self-hosted FreeTAK Server on UK Linux infrastructure. ATAK on crew Android tablets. Cursor on Target (CoT) protocol data sharing between stations, lifeguards, and Coastguard. Operates without internet connectivity once the shore network is established.

FreeTAKATAKUK-hostedCoT protocol

Tier 3: Mesh (last resort)

Meshtastic LoRa mesh nodes at every station — boat-to-boat and boat-to-shore when all other links are down. Battery-operated, self-healing, no internet dependency. Ofcom-compliant frequencies. The tertiary layer that keeps the RNLI communicating when everything else fails.

GNSS independence: the four-layer strategy

GPS is US-operated and can be jammed, spoofed, or selectively degraded. The RNLI's navigation posture must not depend on any single system, any single operator, or any single jurisdiction.

eLoran: no longer a policy aspiration

In November 2025, the UK government committed £71m to the National eLoran Programme — six transmitter sites, initial operating capability January 2027, full CNI resilience by 2029. The planned SW Cornwall transmitter is particularly significant for RNLI Channel and Celtic Sea operations. The RNLI should formally register as an interested maritime stakeholder with the National PNT Office in Phase 1 and engage the General Lighthouse Authorities (who operate the Anthorn transmitter and have deep eLoran expertise) as the natural RNLI connection into the programme.
