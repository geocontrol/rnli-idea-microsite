Purpose of this document

A strategy document, not a procurement list

This microsite presents the strategic technology roadmap for the incoming RNLI Chief Digital and Information Officer. It is grounded in the RNLI's One Crew, One Plan 2026–30 framework and structured around a single governing principle: digital sovereignty in service of the mission to save lives at sea.

Every system, every vendor decision, every data-sharing arrangement is evaluated against two questions: does it serve the mission, and does it keep the RNLI — and the people it serves — safe? The answer to both must be yes before any technology commitment is made.

🇬🇧

The sovereignty doctrine

All RNLI systems, data and operational infrastructure are operated, hosted and supported within the UK. This is not a preference — it is a strategic and operational requirement for a life-safety charity operating in UK and Irish waters. As France formally mandates Linux across 2.5 million government workstations, the technology and political context for this position has never been stronger.

⚓

Save every one — whoever they are

The RNLI's mission is absolute: save lives at sea, regardless of nationality, legal status, or the politics of the journey. The volunteers who crew RNLI lifeboats have been physically targeted for upholding this principle. The digital infrastructure recommended here is designed to protect both the mission and the people who deliver it.

One Crew, One Plan 2026–30

Strategic alignment

Every workstream in this strategy is mapped to the RNLI's three long-term goals and their specific objectives.

Goal 1

Independent volunteering charity & world-renowned rescue service

Digital underpins: crew safety systems, volunteer tools, security posture, value for money, governance and reputation. CDIO owns objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.4 and 1.5.

Goal 2

Deliver a sustainable, future-fit lifesaving service

Digitally-enabled lifesaving. Core platform replacement. Service-led operating model. Fleet technology (sensors, connectivity from 2028). Climate resilience tracking. Process-first, then technology.

Goal 3

Influencer & catalyst for drowning prevention globally, nationally, locally

Open data as evidence for water safety advocacy. Civic Data Commons as the infrastructure for partner sharing. Analytics feeding WHO and UN SDG drowning prevention work.

📋

The delivery model: Simplified + Support

One Crew, One Plan is explicit about how technology must be delivered: simplified processes and operations adopting industry standards, and support services geared towards better-serving the needs of volunteers and lifesavers. This roadmap follows that model exactly: process design comes before technology selection, and every system is evaluated against volunteer usability first.

External validation

The moment is now

Three developments in 2025–26 have transformed the context for this strategy from "ambitious" to "timely."

France mandates Linux across all government

April 2026: France's DINUM formally mandated Linux across 2.5 million government workstations. The Gendarmerie's GendBuntu — 103,000 workstations, 40% TCO reduction — is cited as the governance model. Germany's Schleswig-Holstein: €15m saved on 30,000 workstations. The path is proven.

DINUMGendBuntu€15m saved

UK commits £71m to eLoran

November 2025: the UK government committed £155m to positioning, navigation and timing resilience — £71m specifically for the National eLoran Programme. Six transmitter sites. Initial operating capability targeted January 2027. The RNLI should be an early adopter and maritime advocate now.

Crew safety threats are real and digital

RNLI volunteers involved in Channel rescue operations have been physically targeted and named online by hostile actors. The digital dimension of this threat — crew PII, operational patterns, donor data — requires an explicit threat model and a sovereignty posture that reduces attack surface.

Sector support

CAST: free digital and AI support for charities

CAST (Centre for the Acceleration of Social Technology) is a UK charity that helps social impact organisations make the fullest use of digital and AI — all programmes free of charge.

Their resources map directly onto this roadmap: the AI experimentation canvas for governance of the propensity model, the Digital Toolkit for the volunteer app design process, DigiSafe for digital safeguarding, and the Digital Leads Network for the Linux champion cohort. The RNLI should establish CAST as a named strategic partner in Phase 1.

About this project

A thought experiment

This is a small thought experiment by Mark Simpkins of Geekyoto. The goal was to explore some of the possible directions that the RNLI could consider in revisiting its Data and Technology strategy.

It is not an official RNLI document, not an application for the CDIO role, and not a claim to know the RNLI's internal systems or constraints better than those who work there. It is what it says: a thought experiment — an attempt to think seriously about what digital sovereignty, open-source infrastructure, and human-rights-first data architecture might look like for one of the UK's most cherished and important charities.
